## 2. THE PAGE ARITHMETIC

3000 pages is a real number and it needs a real plan, not a promise.

| Unit | Count | Words each | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chapters | 37 | ~20,000 | ~740,000 |
| Movements per chapter | 5 | ~4,000 | 185 |
| **Manuscript** | | | **~740,000 words** |
| At 250 words / printed page | | | **~2,960 pages** |

Every chapter is composed of **five numbered movements** (I–V). Each movement is a
complete scene-unit with its own turn, hook, and emotional delta. This is deliberate:

- A chapter is never "half-written" — a delivered movement is a finished, publishable unit.
- Drafting can proceed movement-by-movement without the prose turning to summary.
- The 3000-page target is met by *density*, not padding. No filler movements.

## 3. CADENCE

One chapter at a time, as requested.

| Delivery | Contents |
|---|---|
| Chapter 1 (**delivered**, 4,260 w / ~17 pp) | The **calibration chapter**. Voice, heat, syntax, register. Complete and publishable, deliberately short so it can be judged fast. |
| Chapters 2+ | Full 5-movement chapters at ~20,000 w / ~80 pp, one chapter per session |
| Every 5th chapter | Rolling architecture check before continuing |

**Chapter 1 is intentionally the calibration pass.** Read it. If the voice is wrong,
we fix the voice now, not on page 900. If it's right, we lock it and Chapter 2 runs full length.

At ~20,000 words per chapter × 37 chapters, the manuscript completes at ~740,000 words /
~2,960 pages — 98.7% of the 3000-page target, with Chapter 1 the only short unit.

## 6. THE ENDING TRUTH (locked)

The *Belladonna* was scuttled to destroy a decade of fraudulent underwriting records.
Forty-one men drowned to burn a filing cabinet.

Wren Vane was aboard — hidden in the chain locker by her father, the only survivor,
the only living witness to who gave the order.

The log everyone has spent twelve years hunting **does not exist as a document.**
It is a sealed letter in Wren's own nineteen-year-old handwriting, which she does not
remember writing, and which she has always believed was her father's log.

Elliot Crane did not marry a woman who held a paper.
He married the paper.

The marriage that keeps her alive also makes her legally unable to speak.
To tell the truth, she must stop being his wife. To keep him, she must stay silent
and let forty-one men stay drowned.

They choose the truth. They lose the life. They keep each other — mostly.
The reader never gets to be certain that was love and not absolution.
