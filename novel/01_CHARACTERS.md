# PROMPT 2 — CHARACTER PSYCHOLOGY ENGINE
## *The Secret He Married*

---

# I. WREN VANE
**Age** 27 · **Alias** Nell Ash (for eight years) · **Trade** paper conservator, Ash & Quill Conservation
**POV share** ~60% · **Narrator** first person, past tense

### Surface identity
A quiet, exact young woman who repairs damaged paper for a living. Good hands.
Cheap shoes, expensive soap. Says little, notices everything, is unfailingly polite in a way
that keeps people at exactly one arm's length. Colleagues think she is shy. She is not shy.
She is *filing*.

### Private identity
Wren Vane, daughter of the master of the *Belladonna*, raised by the sea's opinion of her
father. She has been running for twelve years and has decided, this autumn, to stop running
and start taking. She has a plan. It is a better plan than Elliot Crane's, and it has been
running longer.

### Buried identity
The girl in the chain locker. Fifteen years old, nine hours in the dark with water coming
up, listening to men she knew call for their mothers. Everything Wren is — the stillness, the
counting, the inability to swim, the way she keeps her shoes on in bed — is that girl
performing a lifelong act of not being her.

### The wound
**Survivor's guilt with a specific shape:** she believes her father chose the *ship* over the
*crew*, and that he chose *her* over both, and that she is therefore the only thing that came
out of that night alive, and that she is not worth it.

### Wound → behaviour (ledger-tracked)
| Trauma behaviour | On the page |
|---|---|
| Hypervigilance | Sits with her back to the wall. Always. Even in her own kitchen. |
| Counting | Counts exits, count of paces, count of breaths. Reader will notice it's always 41 or 42. |
| Cannot swim | Has never told anyone. Will become a plot weapon at Ch 29. |
| Sleep ritual | Shoes on, lamp on, door unlocked. She can't be in a room she can't leave. |
| The left hand | A burn across the palm from a lantern in the locker. Keeps it closed, gloved, or in a pocket. It is her writing hand. |
| Dissociation | Under stress, sound goes to a low hum and her vision narrows to a circle. Written as a prose effect, never labelled. |
| Blackout drinking | Twice a year, alone, in a hotel. She loses hours. This is how the letter happened. |

### Lies she tells (ranked)
1. *"I don't remember anything about that night beyond the noise."* — She remembers everything
   except the eleven minutes that matter. She believes this lie completely. It is not a lie
   she chose; it is one that grew over the hole.
2. *"I'm not here to hurt him."* — She is here to hurt him. She just doesn't know yet how
   much she'll hate doing it.
3. *"The log exists."* — The lie the whole novel rests on. And she has *evidence*: she can
   describe its binding, its buckram, the green ribbon marker, the smell of the ink.
   She has never once been able to quote a single line of it. **Attentive readers will
   catch this on page 40 and not understand it until page 640.**

### Desires / fears / contradictions
- **Wants:** her father's name cleared. To be believed. To stop being a woman with no people.
- **Fears:** deep water. Being taken care of (because care has always preceded a bill).
- **Contradiction:** she despises being owned and she is *aroused* by being chosen.
  She knows exactly how compromised that makes her and she does it anyway. She is not confused
  about herself; she is disappointed in herself, which is worse and more interesting.

### Agency statement (Skill 1: she must not be a passive victim)
Wren is running a con from Chapter 1. She chose Elliot after researching him for eleven months.
She married him because marriage is the only key that opens her father's probate. She is not
rescued into this house; she *infiltrates* it, and then discovers she has infiltrated a trap
that was set for her. Her strength is patience and forgery — she can make paper say anything,
and she has spent twelve years making herself say nothing.

### Micro-details (Zoom-In)
- Touches her collarbone when she is about to lie.
- Signs her alias with a tiny loop on the *l* — a tell she has tried and failed to train out.
- Orders the same thing in every restaurant, then watches what everyone else ordered.
- Keeps a tin of wheat paste flakes in her coat pocket like other women keep cigarettes.
- Freezes for a half-second before stepping onto any boat. Every time. All 37 chapters.

### Arc
| Part | State |
|---|---|
| I | Predator in prey's clothing. Thinks she is the smartest person in the house. |
| II | Learns she is the *only* person in the house not holding a weapon. Gets arrested. |
| III | Stops performing. Remembers. Becomes unbearable to herself, then unbreakable. |
| IV | Chooses to be a witness instead of a wife. Gains the name back. Loses the man for 22 months. |

# II. ELLIOT CRANE
**Age** 38 · **Title** Principal, Crane & Voss Marine Assurance (est. 1881)
**POV share** ~40% · **Narrator** first person, past tense, cooler syntax, longer sentences

### Surface identity
An immaculate, soft-spoken man in grey wool who runs a 143-year-old assurance house and
is widely described, in a town that has known him since he was a boy, as *decent*.
He endows the lifeboat. He knows the names of the widows. He is never late.

### Private identity
The man who signed the survey. At twenty-six, he certified a rust-eaten hull as seaworthy
because his father asked him to and because he wanted, once, to be the son who was trusted.
Forty-one men drowned. He has spent twelve years paying their widows through a charitable
trust with a neutral name and polishing forty-one brass plates every Sunday night.

### Buried identity
The boy on the dock at four in the morning who heard a child's voice on the VHF — one word,
half a name — and did nothing, because doing something meant admitting everything.
He has been listening for that voice for twelve years. **He hears it in Chapter 1 and does
not know he has heard it.** He hears it again in Chapter 15 and knows.

### The wound
**Betrayal trauma, inverted:** he was never betrayed; he was *used*, by a father he loved,
with perfect tenderness, and he said yes. His wound is not that someone hurt him. It is that
he was easy. He cannot forgive himself for being *easy*.

### Wound → behaviour
| Trauma behaviour | On the page |
|---|---|
| Total control of environment | The house is 61°F, always. He irons his own shirts. No one touches his hands. |
| Ceremony as self-punishment | Polishes 41 plates weekly. Times it. It takes him 40 minutes. He has never once hurried. |
| Precision of speech | He never uses a word he can't defend. He calls his wife *Mrs. Crane* for fifteen chapters. |
| Insomnia by tide | Knows the tide table without looking. Sleeps 4 hours. Awake for every low water. |
| Physical tells | Thumb on the rim of a glass, never the stem. Breath goes shallow and even — *too* even — when he lies. |
| Aversion | Cannot say the number forty-two. Will say "forty-one and one." |
| Touch starvation | Has not been touched deliberately in nine years. This is a fuse. |

### Lies he tells
1. *"I married you to protect you."* — True in the way that a cage is a shelter.
2. *"I don't know what happened to the child."* — He does. He has known since 03:12 that night.
   He just doesn't know it was *her*, which is a different and worse ignorance.
3. *"It's finished."* — Everything is finished, always, in Elliot's grammar. Nothing is ever finished.

### Desires / fears / contradictions
- **Wants:** to be absolved without confessing. To be *seen* by one person and not destroyed by it.
- **Fears:** the water giving up what it holds. Being touched. Being forgiven, which he suspects
  he would enjoy too much.
- **Contradiction:** he is a man whose entire life is a controlled burn, and he wants, more than
  he wants safety, for someone to come in with a match. Wren is the match. He knows it. He
  brings her home anyway. **This is his agency — he chooses the fire.**

### Agency statement
Elliot is not a good man made bad by love. He is a compromised man who has paid a subscription
on his own integrity for twelve years, and who sees, in Wren, a way to stop paying — either
by finally burying the thing, or by finally being caught. Both options require her. He chooses
her knowing that.

### Micro-details
- Thumb on the rim, never the stem.
- Folds his receipts into eighths and keeps them. (He has kept every receipt from every
  restaurant he has ever taken her to. This is revealed in Part IV and it is devastating.)
- Sets his watch four minutes fast so he is never late, so he is always, technically, early.
- Says "Thank you" instead of "I love you," and says it too often.
- Takes his coffee standing.
- When he is losing control, his hands go still. Completely still. The stillness is the tell.

### Arc
| Part | State |
|---|---|
| I | Architect. Believes he is choosing. Is being chosen. |
| II | Loses the ability to lie to himself before he loses the ability to lie to her. |
| III | Learns he made her unspeakable. Goes to the one place he can still act — the wreck. |
| IV | Grovel as demolition: gives up firm, name, house, money, freedom. Earns nothing back. Gets something else. |

# III. CORDELIA VOSS — *the mastermind*
**Age** 44 · **Title** Partner, Crane & Voss · **POV share** 0% (she is never a POV; she is weather)

### Surface
A tall grey-eyed woman who dresses like a well-funded archive: charcoal, ivory, nothing shiny.
Chairs the harbor trust. Funds the lifeboat. Sends handwritten notes. Remembers your mother's
name. Universally regarded as the most frightening woman in the county and the only person
who can get the bridge repaired.

### Private
The actuary. In Year −12 the firm was insolvent. She priced the alternative: forty-one lives
against four hundred jobs, a town, and a name that predated her by a century. She chose the
number. She has never once doubted the arithmetic — only its *presentation*. She thinks
Elliot's guilt is a *hygiene* problem. She thinks Wren is *inherited liability*.

### Buried
She was thirty-two. She did not order the *extraction* to fail. She ordered a delay of
forty minutes to keep the *Aster* out of the channel for a coast guard sweep. Forty minutes
is what killed them. This is the detail she has never told anyone, and it is the detail
that makes her, finally, guilty of murder and not merely of fraud.
**She knows this. She has known it for twelve years. She is not sorry about the arithmetic.
She is sorry about the forty minutes — and that sorrow is entirely selfish, because it is
the forty minutes that could hang her.**

### Manipulation toolkit (Skill 4, deployed deliberately)
| Tactic | Where |
|---|---|
| **Isolation** | Marrow Point itself. "You're safer out there." She chose the house. |
| **Love bombing** | Ch 4, then Ch 16, then Ch 21. Each time more generous, each time a hook. |
| **Triangulation** | Ansel. Sedge. The widows. She is always the only sane one in the room. |
| **Breadcrumbing** | Eight years of just enough information to keep Wren looking. |
| **Gaslighting (institutional)** | The firm's records. "There is no such policy." And technically, she's right. |
| **Trauma bonding** | She is the only person who ever tells Wren the truth. This is the cruelty. |
| **Framed protection** | Every restriction is a kindness. Every kindness is a restriction. |

### Micro-details
- Never raises her voice; lowers it.
- Signs documents with a fountain pen she fills herself, in a colour mixed for her.
- Corrects people's grammar when she is deciding whether to ruin them.
- Touches nothing in other people's houses.
- Calls Wren "Nell" in Part I (a test), "Mrs. Crane" in Part II (a leash), "Wren" only once,
  in Chapter 35, and it is the only time she loses.

### Redemption question
**None.** Cordelia does not get a redemption arc. She gets a *legible* one: by the final
chapter the reader understands exactly how a good institution kills forty-one men, and
understands that they would have made the same choice with a spreadsheet in front of them.
That is worse than a villain. That is a mirror.

# IV. BOONE SEDGE
**Age** 51 · **Role** "Marine risk consultant" (fixer) · ex–harbour patrol, twelve years

The kind one. Brings tea. Notices your shoes. Remembers your brother's name and says it
gently. Has killed four people, three of them in ways that were recorded as accidents, and
one of them was Ilya Vane, and he thinks about it more than he thinks about the others
because Ilya was twenty-one and laughed easily.

**The subversion:** Sedge is not the monster in the house. Sedge is the only person in the
novel who is *exactly what he appears to be* — a man doing a job he is good at, for
people who pay him, in a town that would rather not ask. He is the reader's warning that
Cordelia is not aberrant. She is *institutional.*

**Micro-details:** laces his boots left-over-right in a double fisherman's knot. Wren will
notice this in Chapter 18 as the proof he was in the water.
**End:** Chapter 35. He refuses an order for the first time in twelve years, and it kills him.

---

# V. ANSEL VOSS
**Age** 36 · **Role** Cordelia's brother; skipper of the *Aster* at 24 · **The leak**

Beautiful, ruined, funny, drunk by four. He was the man on the rescue vessel that was held
out of the channel for forty minutes. He has spent twelve years being the family's
disgrace precisely so that he would never have to be the family's witness.

**Function:** he is the third obsession — he has been in love with the *memory of a girl in a
locker* since he was twenty-four, because he is the one who heard her on the radio and he is
the one who believed her. When he sees Wren at the winter ball in Chapter 13, he says,
*"You were smaller,"* and the plot detonates.

**Micro-details:** says her father's name wrong on purpose. Drinks from the wrong side of the
glass. Weeps without changing his expression, which is the most frightening thing about him.

---

# VI. MARISOL QUIST
**Age** 63 · **Role** housekeeper, Marrow Point, 30 years (hired by August Crane)

The mirror. A woman who chose silence in 1989 and has been paid in a house ever since.
She is not a servant in the genre sense; she is a co-owner of the silence and she resents
the new wife for making the silence audible.

**Function:** she teaches Wren the house's rules, then breaks one of them for her in Ch 24.
She is the novel's argument that survival and complicity are the same word spelled differently.
**Micro-details:** irons the newspaper before Elliot reads it. Has never once sat down in the
dining room. Keeps her own tea in her own tin, and Wren notices, and understands everything.

---

# VII. EUNICE TARRANT
**Age** 70 · **Role** solicitor, Tarrant & Quill; holds the sealed letter

Bound, precise, and genuinely decent — which is what makes her terrifying. She will not break
a confidence, ever, and the whole plot turns on that. She is the only character who never lies.

**Micro-details:** keeps a clock in her office that is eleven minutes slow and refuses to have
it fixed, because her father set it that way. Wren will fix it in Chapter 26 and Tarrant will
finally tell her the truth. *The clock is the key to Tarrant.*

---

# VIII. BEX ADEYEMI
**Age** 29 · **Role** conservator, Wren's friend and colleague · **The chorus**

Says the reader's worries out loud: *"That's not a marriage, Wren, that's a conservatorship."*
Loves Wren without understanding her, which is the most useful kind of love in a thriller.
**Function:** the outside perspective that proves the isolation is working.
**Risk:** she is the collateral. The reader should spend Part III afraid for her.

---

# IX. THE DEAD (present in every chapter)

- **TOBIAS VANE** (d. 55) — master of the *Belladonna*. Deliberately grounded her on Gannet
  Rock so the paper would survive the sinking. Chose the archive and his daughter over the
  possibility of saving his crew. Wren does not know this until Chapter 27.
  *He is not a hero. He is a man who made a choice at 02:31 and lived four more minutes.*
- **NADIA VANE** (d. 43) — Wren's mother. Knew. Was going to talk. Fell.
- **ILYA VANE** (d. 21) — Wren's brother. Was going to talk. Drowned.
- **AUGUST CRANE** (d. 61) — Elliot's father. Ordered the scuttling. Died eleven days after
  the claim was paid, having said, in eleven days, not one word about it.
- **HALE VOSS** (d. 70) — Cordelia and Ansel's father. Built the fraud. Was never told.

**At Chapter 1:** Elliot believes he is the predator and Wren is the asset.
Wren believes she is the predator and Elliot is the key. Cordelia knows they are both assets.
Sedge knows what Wren sounds like. Ansel doesn't know she's alive. Marisol knows everything
and has said nothing for thirty years. Tarrant holds the only true document in the novel
and has been forbidden to mention that it exists.
