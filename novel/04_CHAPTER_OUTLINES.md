# PROMPT 6 — 37-CHAPTER ARCHITECTURE ENGINE
## *The Secret He Married*

**Total target:** ~740,000 words · 37 chapters × 5 movements × ~4,000 words · ~2,960 pp.
**POV:** Alternating first person, past tense. WREN ~60% / ELLIOT ~40%.
**Rule:** every chapter = 5 movements (I–V). Every movement = one turn of the screw.
**No filler chapters. Every chapter must do at least three of: raise pressure, deepen obsession,
reveal, shift power.**

---

# PART I — THE OFFER
### *Chapters 1–9 · 17 October – 30 November · The brain of this part is ACQUISITION*

---

## CHAPTER 1 — THE WIFE IN THE FOG
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 1–80
**Purpose:** Establish the marriage as a transaction already completed; establish Marrow Point as a
cage with a view; make the reader complicit in a wedding they can feel is a purchase.
**Conflict:** Wren vs. the fact that she has just done the most irreversible thing of her life and
cannot undo it until the tide allows.
**Revelation (to reader):** the marriage is four hours old and neither party has told the other
one true thing. The bride knows her husband signed the survey that killed her father.
**Relationship delta:** stranger → spouse, legally. Emotionally: adversary → adversary with a
shared address.
**Power shift:** nominal — Elliot holds everything. Actual — Wren is inside the walls now.
**Clues planted:** the blank 42nd plate; her closed left hand; the half-second before she steps
onto the boat; he says "Mrs. Crane" and it sounds like a filing instruction.
**Mastermind activity:** offstage. Cordelia has arranged the weather of this day — she chose the
registrar, the time, the tide. Wren notices the registrar was Cordelia's solicitor and thinks
nothing of it.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the locked northwest door. Payoff — Ch 11.
**Ending propulsion:** she sees the 41 plates and one empty nail, and counts. Forty-two.
**Movements:** I. The registry office, three witnesses, no flowers. II. The cab, the harbor, the fog.
III. The boat; the first conversation that is all subtext. IV. Landfall; the house from the water.
V. The hall; the sound of him locking a door that isn't the front one.

## CHAPTER 2 — THIRTY-ONE DAYS
**POV** Elliot · **Pp** 81–160
**Purpose:** Give the predator's arithmetic. Show the origin of the obsession (Skill: the Why-Now
Trigger — the Recognition Moment) and make the plan legible, mechanical, and almost sympathetic.
**Conflict:** Elliot vs. his own competence. The plan works perfectly and that is the problem.
**Revelation:** he chose her after seeing her hands on a damaged ledger in September; he has
believed for eleven months that he found her.
**Relationship delta:** asset → person-with-a-name. He notices and does not stop noticing.
**Power shift:** he loses the first inch — he lies to Tarrant, which he has never done.
**Clues:** the eighth-folded receipts begin; he cannot say "wife"; the word "log" makes his hands
go still; he remembers a child's voice on a VHF at 03:12 and describes it as *noise*.
**Mastermind activity:** Cordelia, in one scene, suggests the marriage. Elliot believes he thought
of it. **The reader is shown this and will not register it until Ch 25.**
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the survey certificate, framed, in his study (Ch 9). Payoff — Ch 22.
**Ending propulsion:** he stands outside her door on the first night and hears her counting, and
recognises the rhythm.
**Movements:** I. September, the conservation lab, her hands. II. November, the file on his desk.
III. December (out of order), the meeting with Cordelia where the idea is planted. IV. The
morning of the wedding, ironing a shirt. V. The corridor at Marrow Point, listening.

## CHAPTER 3 — THE HOUSE THAT FACES THE WRECK
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 161–240
**Purpose:** Map the cage. Every room faces out. Teach the reader the tide clock.
**Conflict:** Wren vs. Marisol Quist, who will not let her move a single object.
**Revelation:** the house was bought eleven years ago and has never held a guest.
**Relationship delta:** she learns the rules of his house and the shape of his grief.
**Power shift:** Marisol. The servant is the only person who can say no.
**Clues:** she describes the log's *binding* in detail and quotes nothing (present tense creeps in);
a hollow in the signal-station wall she is not yet allowed to open; nine windows; no mirrors
facing the sea.
**Mastermind activity:** a letter arrives from Cordelia, addressed to **"Mrs. Crane,"** posted
*before* the wedding.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the tide table on the kitchen wall. Payoff — Ch 29.
**Ending propulsion:** she finds the northwest door. It is not locked. It is *painted shut.*
**Movements:** I. Waking in an unfamiliar bed with her shoes on. II. Breakfast; the rules.
III. The tour; nine windows. IV. Marisol's tea tin. V. The painted door.

## CHAPTER 4 — GRACE BEFORE MEAT
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 241–320
**Purpose:** Introduce Cordelia at full power. Establish that this marriage is an event in a
corporate calendar, not a private life.
**Conflict:** three-way breakfast. Elliot and Cordelia speak in actuarial shorthand; Wren is the
only person at the table who doesn't work there and is the only one who understands everything.
**Revelation:** Cordelia calls her **"Nell."** Nobody at that table was told the name Nell.
**Relationship delta:** Wren realizes her husband's partner has been watching her longer than her
husband has.
**Power shift:** Cordelia, permanently and by a mile.
**Clues:** Cordelia brings a gift — a book press, second-hand, from a shop that shut in 2014,
which is the year Wren first used that shop. Cordelia corrects Elliot's grammar (she is deciding
something about him). Cordelia touches nothing.
**Mastermind activity:** full scene. She is sizing up her asset and finding it **better than
expected** — which is the first time the reader should feel afraid *for* Cordelia.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — "Nell." Payoff — Ch 4 ends it; Ch 21 explains it.
**Ending propulsion:** Cordelia, leaving, to Wren: *"You'll want to keep a diary. Everyone does,
out here."*
**Movements:** I. The launch arriving at first light. II. The table; the arithmetic. III. The book
press; the wrong name. IV. A walk on the headland, two women, no witnesses. V. The car on the
causeway, and the water already over the road.

## CHAPTER 5 — THE RULES OF THE HOUSE
**POV** Elliot · **Pp** 321–400
**Purpose:** Let him lay down law that is transparently a map of his guilt. Let her break one
rule before the chapter ends.
**Conflict:** He needs her predictable. She is a conservator; she understands that a controlled
environment is what preserves a thing, and she refuses to be preserved.
**Revelation:** he has never let anyone sleep under this roof. And: the tide table is not on the
wall for the boats.
**Relationship delta:** from spouses to *occupants of a jurisdiction.*
**Power shift:** she breaks the tide rule and he does not punish her — he goes pale. First crack.
**Clues:** he sets his watch four minutes fast in front of her; he says "forty-one and one" and
she doesn't ask; he lists nine rules and there are only eight reasons.
**Mastermind activity:** a call from Cordelia he takes outside, in the rain, and lies about.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — Rule Six, "you do not go below the tide line." Payoff — Ch 12.
**Ending propulsion:** she goes below the tide line and finds a door in the rock.
**Movements:** I. The nine rules, delivered over coffee, standing. II. She asks why. He answers
honestly for eleven words. III. The afternoon; her first attempt to work; her hands shake.
IV. Low water; the causeway; she walks out too far. V. The door in the rock, and the tide coming.

## CHAPTER 6 — A KIND MAN
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 401–480
**Purpose:** Introduce the novel's physical threat as a *courtesy*. Teach the reader to fear tea.
**Conflict:** Wren vs. Boone Sedge, who is arranging her bookshelf and telling her about her
brother.
**Revelation:** Sedge knew Ilya. Sedge liked Ilya. Sedge was on the causeway the night Ilya died.
**Relationship delta:** Wren ↔ Elliot: she asks him to send the man away; he says he can't.
**Power shift:** Sedge, absolutely. In the room, he is the only one who has ever killed anyone.
**Clues:** she recognises his boots before she sees his face; his left boot laces left-over-right;
he brings tea already made, the way you do for someone whose hands shake.
**Mastermind activity:** none — Sedge is Cordelia's hand, and hands do not plot.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the bootlace. Payoff — Ch 18.
**Ending propulsion:** he leaves. She goes to the window. He is standing at the edge of the
light, at the head of the path, looking up. He does not wave.
**Movements:** I. A car on the causeway at an hour the causeway shouldn't be open. II. Tea.
III. Ilya, remembered by a stranger. IV. The library; the staggering of shelves. V. The window.

## CHAPTER 7 — WHAT THE TIDE LEFT
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 481–560
**Purpose:** Deliver the object of desire. Make the reader want the log as badly as she does.
**Conflict:** probate has released nothing for twelve years; marriage has released it all at once,
in cardboard, in a hatchback.
**Revelation:** her father's effects come home. His brass keys. His oilskin. A child's shoe she
doesn't recognise. And a tide almanac with three dates circled.
**Relationship delta:** Wren ↔ her father. She likes him less by the end of the chapter and
doesn't know why.
**Power shift:** Wren — she now owns something Elliot needs. Neither says so.
**Clues:** she can describe the log's buckram and ribbon from twelve years of *repetition*, not
reading; the almanac's three dates are Y−12 3 October, Y−9 14 April, Y−4 22 August — the nights
her father, her mother and her brother died. **Three dates, three dead Vanes. It is not a tide
map; it is a list of people who were watched.**
**Mastermind activity:** the probate release happens faster than any probate release in the
history of the county. Cordelia made a phone call. Wren thinks it was her own cleverness.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the almanac. Payoff — Ch 24.
**Ending propulsion:** the shoe is small. It is not hers. Somebody's child was on that ship.
**Movements:** I. The hatchback; four boxes. II. The oilskin; the smell. III. The keys, one at a
time, and what each one opens. IV. The shoe. V. The almanac, and the three circles, and her hands
going cold in a warm room.

## CHAPTER 8 — NINE FATHOM
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 561–640
**Purpose:** First intimacy, and nothing happens. Establish that the danger in this house is not
violence; it is being seen.
**Conflict:** the storm takes the power; the causeway floods; for four hours there is no way off
the point and no way to pretend they are not alone.
**Revelation:** he is afraid of her. That is the first true thing in the book and it is his.
**Relationship delta:** adversary → the person who is in the dark with you.
**Power shift:** Wren. In the dark, the man who owns everything cannot see.
**Clues:** he knows exactly how long the blackout will last; he has candles *pre-positioned*; he
says her name — "Nell" — and it is the last time he uses it before Ch 19.
**Mastermind activity:** none. This is the only chapter in Part I with no hand on it, and that is
why it feels safe, and that is why it isn't.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — "Mrs. Crane." Payoff — Ch 15.
**Ending propulsion:** the power comes back at 02:40. They are four inches apart. He gets up,
says *"Goodnight, Mrs. Crane,"* and she lies awake until dawn counting.
**Movements:** I. The glass going; the sea standing up. II. Candles he did not have to look for.
III. The blanket; the four inches; the conversation that is all subtext. IV. He tells her about
the plates, and not about the blank one. V. 02:40.

## CHAPTER 9 — THE INSTRUMENT
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 641–720
**Purpose:** PART I CLIMAX. She learns she is a deed. She chooses to stay. The con becomes a
marriage becomes a war.
**Conflict:** Wren vs. the document.
**Revelation:** the Vane Instrument — *to my daughter upon her lawful marriage, to be held jointly
with her husband as her lord and protector.* Every kindness in Part I rereads as acquisition.
**Relationship delta:** she stops being his wife and becomes his counterparty.
**Power shift:** the flip of Part I. She knows. He doesn't know she knows.
**Clues:** Tarrant's clock, eleven minutes slow; the word "protector" in a dead man's hand; the
survey certificate framed in the study, dated four weeks before the sinking — *he was on that ship
before it went down.*
**Mastermind activity:** the clause was drafted by **Crane & Voss's own solicitors**. The trap
was built by her enemy, gifted to her father, signed by a dying man. Wren does not yet see the
signature on the drafting note. **It is there. It is Cordelia's initial.**
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the drafting note. Payoff — Ch 25.
**Ending propulsion:** she does not cry. She goes to her workroom, lays out her tools, and begins
to make something. She is going to forge the log.
**Movements:** I. The errand; the wet afternoon in the city. II. Tarrant's office; the clock.
III. The instrument, read aloud, in a stranger's voice. IV. The walk to the harbor; the arithmetic
of staying. V. The workroom; the tools laid out in a line; the first cut of the blade.

## CHAPTER 10 — COLD WAR
**POV** Elliot · **Pp** 721–800
**Purpose:** The duel begins. Two people who know each other's crime, sharing a bathroom.
**Conflict:** He knows she knows. He cannot ask. Asking is confession.
**Revelation (to Elliot):** she has been in his study. The survey certificate has been moved one
inch and rehung at the wrong height.
**Relationship delta:** spouses → intelligence services.
**Power shift:** Wren. She has moved from asset to adversary and he is *thrilled*, which disgusts
him, which is the chapter.
**Clues:** he starts sleeping in the chair in the corridor; the receipts are now daily; he can
smell her paste on the air and it makes his hands go still.
**Mastermind activity:** Cordelia requests the marriage certificate be lodged with the firm "for
safekeeping." Elliot agrees. **This is the Reverter's fuse being lit and nobody in the room knows.**
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the certificate. Payoff — Ch 25/30.
**Ending propulsion:** he goes to the study at 3 a.m. and finds the certificate moved *again* —
and this time there's a hair between the frame and the wall.
**Movements:** I. Breakfast in silence, two newspapers. II. The study; the one inch. III. A
conversation about the weather that is a negotiation about everything. IV. The corridor chair.
V. The hair.

## CHAPTER 11 — THE COUNTERFEIT
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 801–880
**Purpose:** Give her a weapon and make it a lie. Establish her craft as power and as symptom.
**Conflict:** Wren vs. her own hands. She can make the paper say anything. She has done it before.
**Revelation:** she *did* do it before. At nineteen. She made a beginning and stopped. She
remembers stopping and does not remember why.
**Relationship delta:** she begins to need him to be deceived, which is a form of dependence.
**Power shift:** Wren — she has a lever.
**Clues:** she works from *memory of the object*, never from contents; she has to invent the
words; the invented words sound like her father's voice and she doesn't notice that she's writing
him; the green ribbon is the exact shade of the ribbon on the boxes of his effects, because it
came from the same shop, because she bought it.
**Mastermind activity:** Sedge, on the causeway, watching the light in her workroom window. He
reports it. Cordelia is pleased. **A forger in the house is useful to her.**
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the half-finished original. Payoff — Ch 19.
**Ending propulsion:** she finishes the first page and reads it back and thinks: *I have heard
this before.*
**Movements:** I. The painted door, opened with a knife. II. The room behind it: a cot, a lamp,
and nothing else. III. The materials; the discipline; the craft explained. IV. The words, invented.
V. The page, read aloud, and the present tense creeping in.

## CHAPTER 12 — THE FOG LOG
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 881–960
**Purpose:** First hard proof. Move the thriller from suspicion to evidence.
**Conflict:** Rule Six. She breaks it and it is worth it.
**Revelation:** the signal station's official meteorological log for the night of the sinking has
one line altered — visibility "nil" overwritten to "moderate," in a different ink.
**Relationship delta:** from adversary to *witness against him.* She is now the only person alive
who can hurt him, and she finds she doesn't want to, which is the first sign of the disease.
**Power shift:** Wren, decisively, for four chapters.
**Clues:** the correction is in Elliot's hand — she has seen his hand on nine household lists; the
station's keeper, Old Pell, is still up there and has never been asked a question in twelve years;
the log's binding is identical to the binding of the log she has been forging. **Identical,
because she bought the same stock.**
**Mastermind activity:** Pell mentions a woman who came asking, years ago, about the same page.
He describes her coat. It is Cordelia's coat.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — Pell. Payoff — Ch 36.
**Ending propulsion:** she photographs the page. The flash goes off. Somewhere below, a door opens.
**Movements:** I. The door in the rock, and the tunnel, and the climb. II. Old Pell; the stove;
the fog log. III. The altered line. IV. Pell's coat story. V. The flash; the door below.

## CHAPTER 13 — THE ASTER'S CAPTAIN
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 961–1040
**Purpose:** Introduce Ansel. Detonate the first structural charge: someone knows her face.
**Conflict:** public performance of a happy marriage in a room full of people who signed the claim.
**Revelation:** Ansel Voss says, *"You were smaller."*
**Relationship delta:** Elliot ↔ Wren: he sees Ansel see her, and he moves — physically, in front
of eighty people — and the reader understands he is already lost.
**Power shift:** Ansel. He has a memory nobody else has.
**Clues:** Ansel calls her father **"Toby"**; Ansel drinks from the wrong side of the glass; Ansel
weeps without changing expression; Cordelia watches her brother watch Wren and *smiles* — she has
been waiting four years for this.
**Mastermind activity:** Cordelia invited Ansel deliberately. She is using her brother as a lit
match to see what burns.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — "Toby." Payoff — Ch 24.
**Ending propulsion:** Ansel, on the stairs, hand on her wrist: *"Did he tell you he was there?"*
**Movements:** I. The dress; the corsetry of being Mrs. Crane. II. The ballroom; the widows.
III. The introduction; "You were smaller." IV. The dance; the whisper. V. The stairs.

## CHAPTER 14 — THE CLAIM
**POV** Elliot · **Pp** 1041–1120
**Purpose:** MIDPOINT A. External clock. Both of them now have sixty days.
**Conflict:** a salvage company files to lift the *Belladonna*. Elliot must stop it without
appearing to want to stop it.
**Revelation:** the salvor's survey maps show the forward hold intact — the archive is *there*,
readable, twelve years on, in cold water, in a sealed hold. Paper can survive in a sealed hold.
**Relationship delta:** he needs her consent. The one thing he cannot buy, steal, or forge is
the signature of the master's heir, and she is beginning to understand that.
**Power shift:** Wren. She doesn't know it yet. **The reader does.**
**Clues:** the salvor is funded through a trust with a name Elliot doesn't recognise — and the
reader will not either until Ch 25; the filing names "the Vane interest" as a consenting party,
and nobody has asked the Vane interest anything.
**Mastermind activity:** Cordelia manufactured this claim. She needs the deadline. She is now
running a clock on three people at once.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — consent. Payoff — Ch 29 and Ch 36.
**Ending propulsion:** he goes home to ask his wife for a signature and finds her workroom door
open and a page drying on the table, and the handwriting is her father's.
**Movements:** I. The notice of claim, read three times. II. The survey maps; the forward hold.
III. Cordelia's office; the arithmetic of stopping it. IV. The causeway; the dread of the ask.
V. The workroom door.

## CHAPTER 15 — THE BARGAIN
**POV** Wren → Elliot (shifting gaze at the vulnerability point) · **Pp** 1121–1200
**Purpose:** MIDPOINT B. First full intimacy. Power flips. The word "wife" lands.
**Conflict:** she offers the signature in exchange for access to the wreck files. The price she
names is not the price he pays.
**Revelation:** she does not want the money, or the files, or the leverage. She wants to be
*chosen by someone who knows what she is.* She says it and hates herself.
**Relationship delta:** from counterparties to *lovers who are still lying*, which is the most
dangerous configuration in the book.
**Power shift:** her, then him, then her. The only even chapter in the novel.
**Clues:** he calls her **"my wife"** for the first time and she goes cold in his arms and he
feels it and says nothing; spousal privilege is *planted here* as a joke he makes about a
business dispute, and she laughs, and neither of them knows.
**Mastermind activity:** none on the page. Cordelia's absence in this chapter is the loudest
thing in it.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — "my wife." Payoff — Ch 28.
**Ending propulsion:** after, in the dark, she asks him to say it again and he says *"Mrs. Crane,"*
and she understands that he has taken it back, and that he is afraid of the word, and that she
now has something that will destroy him that has nothing to do with paper.
**Movements:** I. The offer; the negotiation; the terms. II. The refusal. III. The second offer,
which is the honest one. IV. *(gaze shift)*. V. The withdrawal of the word.

## CHAPTER 16 — THE WIDOWS' LEDGER
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 1201–1280
**Purpose:** Cordelia's recruitment. Show the reader the machinery of twelve years of silence.
**Conflict:** Wren vs. the best offer she has ever been made.
**Revelation:** Elliot has been paying forty-one households through a trust for twelve years.
It is not charity. It is a subscription.
**Relationship delta:** Wren ↔ Cordelia: the beginning of the only honest relationship in the book,
which is with the villain.
**Power shift:** Cordelia — she hands Wren a key and a truth and a door, and all three are hooks.
**Clues:** the trust's name is the name of a street where Wren's mother died; Bex appears and says
the line about the second locked room; the ledger has forty-one lines and a forty-second line
ruled but empty, in Cordelia's hand. **She has been waiting for Wren to fill it.**
**Mastermind activity:** full. She is separating Wren from Elliot with truth, which is the one
weapon nobody can defend against.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the empty forty-second line. Payoff — Ch 35.
**Ending propulsion:** Cordelia: *"He has never told you what he was doing at 03:12, has he."*
**Movements:** I. The city; the fourth floor; the room where the claim was signed. II. The ledger.
III. The arithmetic explained, kindly. IV. Bex, on the street, saying the true thing. V. The lift
down, and the question in the mirrored doors.

## CHAPTER 17 — THE GIRL IN THE LOCKER
**POV** Elliot · **Pp** 1281–1360
**Purpose:** The almost-confession. The closest the book gets to honesty before Chapter 22.
**Conflict:** he is going to tell her. He has decided. The decision is the intimacy.
**Revelation (partial):** a child was aboard. He heard her on the VHF. He has never told anyone,
including himself, in twelve years.
**Relationship delta:** he offers her the only thing he has; she cannot take it, because taking it
would end her plan.
**Power shift:** Wren — she stops him. And the reader realises *she* is the one with something
to hide now.
**Clues:** he imitates the voice — one word, half a name; she goes very still and touches her
collarbone; the phone rings; the interruption is **Sedge**, and the reader knows what Sedge is,
and Elliot doesn't.
**Mastermind activity:** the phone call is Cordelia's, timed. She knows what he was about to say.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the half-name. Payoff — Ch 26.
**Ending propulsion:** he answers the phone. It is Cordelia. She says three words and he leaves
the house at midnight and does not come back until Thursday.
**Movements:** I. The two of them, sober, in a warm kitchen, at the worst possible moment. II. The
beginning of it. III. The voice on the radio, imitated. IV. Her stillness. V. The telephone.

## CHAPTER 18 — LACED WRONG
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 1361–1440
**Purpose:** The first body. Convert a legal thriller into a survival thriller.
**Conflict:** the salvage surveyor is dead, and the death is a diving accident, and it is not.
**Revelation:** his boots are laced wrong. Nobody laces a dead man's boots for him.
**Relationship delta:** Wren ↔ Sedge: she has proof of what he is; he knows she has it; the
politeness between them becomes the most frightening thing in the book.
**Power shift:** Sedge. He stops pretending.
**Clues:** the double fisherman's knot; the surveyor had a copy of the forward-hold map in his
truck; the map is gone; Sedge brings her tea that evening, and the tea is the right temperature,
and she drinks it, and the reader screams.
**Mastermind activity:** Cordelia did not order this. **Sedge did.** He is protecting himself, and
that makes him the first thing in the novel operating outside her arithmetic — the first sign she
is losing control of her own hand.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the missing map. Payoff — Ch 24.
**Ending propulsion:** she finishes the tea. He takes the cup. He says: *"Your brother used to
lace his boots like that. Terrible habit. I told him."*
**Movements:** I. The harbor at dawn; the police tape. II. The body; the boots. III. The knot.
IV. The interview with the constable, in which she says nothing. V. Tea.

## CHAPTER 19 — THE FORGED THING
**POV** Elliot · **Pp** 1441–1520
**Purpose:** PART II CLIMAX. His darkest act. He chooses silence and lets her be arrested.
**Conflict:** Cordelia produces the half-finished forgery — Wren's original, from when she was
nineteen — and puts it in front of the salvage court as evidence of fraud by "the Vane interest."
**Revelation:** the document Elliot has hunted for twelve years is a forgery, and the forger is
his wife, and she has been lying to him since the first night.
**Relationship delta:** husband → witness for the prosecution, by omission.
**Power shift:** Elliot. He has one move. He uses it. It is the wrong one.
**Clues:** he recognises the handwriting — not because it's Tobias's, but because of the **loop on
the l**. He has seen it on a wedding register. He says nothing.
**Mastermind activity:** Cordelia's design. She needs Wren discredited *and* Elliot complicit.
She gets both in one afternoon and does not smile once.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the loop. Payoff — Ch 26 and Ch 37.
**Ending propulsion:** they take her out through the yard. He stands in the gallery. She looks at
him. He does not move. **That is the last image of Part II: a man who does not move.**
**Movements:** I. The hearing; the room; the dignities of the law. II. The exhibit; the loop.
III. The choice, made in four seconds, in a man who has never made a fast decision in his life.
IV. The yard; the van. V. Her face through glass, and his hands absolutely still.

## CHAPTER 20 — REMAND
**POV** Wren · **Pp** 1521–1600
**Purpose:** Strip her of everything. Establish the cell as the locker again.
**Conflict:** Wren vs. confinement, and confinement is the one thing she cannot survive twice.
**Revelation:** she is charged not with fraud but with *conspiracy to defraud the underwriter* —
a charge that exists because Crane & Voss made a complaint. **Her husband's firm is prosecuting
her.** She learns this from a newspaper, not a lawyer.
**Relationship delta:** from wife to defendant. The word "wife" appears nowhere in the indictment.
**Power shift:** nobody. Total powerlessness, for one chapter only, and she uses it.
**Clues:** the cell's dimensions; she counts. The dock is 4.4 metres by 2.1. She counts 42 tiles.
She cannot swim and the walls are painted the colour of water.
**Mastermind activity:** Cordelia has not visited. She is waiting for the precise hour of maximum
despair, which she calculates to the day.
**Setup/payoff:** setup — the 42 tiles. Payoff — Ch 27.
**Ending propulsion:** a warder calls a name, and it isn't hers, and it is: **"Vane."** They have
stopped calling her Crane.
